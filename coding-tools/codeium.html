Lesson 01 — Foundations

Codeium, the free everyday copilot

Codeium is a code completion and chat assistant that plugs into your existing editor, offering fast inline suggestions and a generous free tier for individual developers.

01.1 What makes Codeium different

Where many AI coding tools charge per seat from day one, Codeium built its reputation on a strong, genuinely free individual plan — full-featured autocomplete and chat, not a crippled trial. It supports dozens of languages and integrates into editors like VS Code, JetBrains IDEs, and Vim, rather than asking you to switch to a new one.

It's less about agentic, multi-file automation and more about being a fast, unobtrusive layer over the editor you already use daily.

01.2 Where it fits in your workflow

Inline autocomplete

Multi-line suggestions as you type, tuned to match the style already in your file.

In-editor chat

Ask questions about your codebase without leaving the IDE or switching windows.

Individual developers

A strong free tier makes it a natural first AI tool for students and solo builders.

Multi-IDE teams

Useful where teammates use different editors, since support spans most major ones.

01.3 The one mindset shift that matters

Use it as a typing accelerant, not a planner. Codeium shines when it can guess your next few lines from strong local context — keep functions focused and well-named, and its suggestions get noticeably sharper.

With the fundamentals set, Lesson 2 covers how to get consistently good completions.

Lesson 02 — Practice

Getting sharper suggestions

Small habits that noticeably improve completion quality, and a few things that quietly hurt it.

02.1 A five-step routine

  1. 01 Write a clear function signature and docstring first — completions lean heavily on both.
  2. 02 Keep related files open in your editor; Codeium reads nearby open tabs for extra context.
  3. 03 Accept suggestions incrementally rather than in one long block, so you can correct drift early.
  4. 04 Use the chat panel for "why" questions instead of trying to coax an explanation out of autocomplete.
  5. 05 Periodically clear stale, half-finished code nearby — it can bias future suggestions.

02.2 A small example

A docstring that reliably steers good completions:

def parse_csv_row(row: list[str]) -> dict:
    """Convert a CSV row into a dict using the module-level
    HEADER list as keys, casting numeric fields to float."""
    # Codeium will now suggest a body matching this contract

The signature, types, and docstring together give Codeium a precise contract to fill in — far more reliable than an empty function with just a name.

02.3 Mistakes to avoid

Accepting on autopilot

Tab-accepting every suggestion without reading it lets subtle logic errors slip in unnoticed.

No local context

Writing in a brand-new empty file gives the model little to anchor its suggestions to.

Expecting agentic edits

It's not built to restructure a whole codebase on its own — use it for line and function-level help.

Ignoring the chat panel

Many users only use autocomplete and miss the chat feature, which is better for reasoning tasks.

Codeium rewards clarity at the point of writing — a good signature and comment upfront save several rounds of correction later.
